# Words and patterns to avoid

These add length without meaning, or mark text as machine-written. Each one has a plain replacement or can be deleted.

## Words

delve, dive into, navigate (figurative), underscore, bolster, foster, harness, leverage, unpack, shed light on, pave the way, pivotal, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, transformative, game-changing, game-changer, innovative, revolutionary, robust, comprehensive, seamless, intricate, nuanced (as praise), vibrant, multifaceted, holistic, synergy, synergistic, testament, landscape (figurative), realm, streamline, empower, utilize/utilise, elevate, unlock, unleash, supercharge, unprecedented, paradigm, world-class, best-in-class, no-brainer, quietly (as drama), honestly (as a qualifier).

Use "is" instead of "serves as", "featuring" or "boasting".

Don't minimise difficulty: obviously, simply, just (as in "just do X"), as you know, of course.

## Filler phrases

Delete these or say the plain thing instead:

- "It's important to note that", "It's worth noting that", "Worth exploring/considering"
- "When it comes to", "At its core", "At the end of the day", "In today's fast-paced world"
- "This is where X comes in", "Let's break it down", "Here's the thing", "Here's where it gets interesting"
- "Plays a crucial role in", "One of the most important", "It cannot be overstated"
- "This matters because", "The honest answer is", "The real problem is"
- "In other words", "Put differently", "To summarise"
- "Please be advised", "Kindly note", "Going forward", "At this moment in time", "Circle back", "Touch base", "Unfortunately", "We regret to inform you"
- "Great question!", "Exciting news", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you have any questions"

## Structures

- "It's not just X, it's Y", "Not only X, but also Y", "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- "No X. No Y. Just Z."
- A short, quotable line at the end of every paragraph.
- Two clauses with the same shape and a few words swapped ("Good code does A; bad code does B").
- A made-up journey of discovery ("the question I keep coming back to").
- Ranking your own points ("most importantly", "the key insight here").
- Agreeing and then promising more ("You're right, and here's the exact form"). Give the better version directly.
- Dramatic headings ("The pricing trap"). Use plain labels ("Pricing").
- Restating the question before answering it, or listing what you are about to say.

## Formatting

- No em-dashes.
- No horizontal rules between paragraphs.
- No emoji unless the user asks for them or the template uses them.
- No all-caps for emphasis. Bold sparingly, for the one thing the reader must not miss.
- Avoid exclamation marks.
